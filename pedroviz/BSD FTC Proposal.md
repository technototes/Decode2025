# BSD FTC Proposal

## Problem Statement

There are only 2 schools in the Bellevue School District that have active FTC
programs. There are _numerous_ community teams. While there is certainly nothing
wrong with building and supporting a community team, there are real problems
that come with community teams:

#### Inclusion and Equity

Community teams have a completely understandable negative: In order to join a
community team, you typically need to have some existing relationship with
someone on the community team. In addition, there are no rules or expectations
around accomodating students with any degree of special needs. At best, many
community teams are simply not equipped to handle students who may have specific
needs. At worse, some community teams may be hostile to those students.

#### Funding

Community teams, being community formed, are generally not able to pull off a
tax-exempt fundraising mechanism, which reduces their overall ability to fund
through donations, so they typically are self-funded.

#### Sustainability

Community teams are _generally_ supported by a primary student and their parent.
They tend to end once that student graduates.

## Proposed Solution

Within the Bellevue School District, there are only 2 schools with FTC teams:
International School, a "choice" school (lottery) with a total student count of
less than 700, and Sammamish High School, with a smaller student body than the
other 3 general high schools, but with a well equipped work shop on premise.
Expanding the Sammamish High School FTC team to be available to all students
across the district would enable both a more sustainable team (as the team is
currently struggling with the reducing student population of SHS), as well as
offer FTC to _all_ BSD students, not just the few who happen to attend SHS or
are able to attend IS.

1. Create a central, inclusive FTC team for all BSD high school students.

### Non-Goals

- We're not competing with community teams to try to 'outperform' them (or any

  we have several students who are members of 2412, the Robototes, it's expected
  that FTC members may also be active members of their local high school's FRC
  team.
